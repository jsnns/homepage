_This is a follow up to [How We Build 2.0](/thoughts/how-we-build-2-0) after working on [Spawn](https://spawn.co) for a few months._

> When AI handles every email, writes every document, and runs every business, what's left for humans? Not tasks. Not productivity. _Creation_.

---

## Something Durable

For the past while I've been trying to answer one question: what can I build that's durable to as many futures as I can imagine? Fast takeoff, sovereign AI, AI that codes everything, a plateau, mass unemployment, business as usual. The world is very likely to change a lot—my working assumption is that we have only a few years of normalcy left—and that assumption kills most ideas on contact.

A SaaS product assumes there's some productive inefficiency to eke out with software—an automation, an organization of data. That makes no sense in a world where agent-to-agent business is the norm. Claude's gonna run every business, and Claude-to-Claude business doesn't need SaaS. Dev tools fail for the same reason: tools for productivity don't make sense when productivity is no longer a primary human aim.

This isn't theoretical for me. I've been coding with LLMs since GPT-4 and Sonnet 3.5, and I'm building the first version of the game engine on Opus 4 right now. It doesn't work very well—but it works well enough that I can see where it's going.

So software is splitting into two futures: AI-operated utilities that run the world, and human experiences that make it worth living in. The first category is already being claimed. OpenAI and Anthropic are building the AI that will do your job—agents that manage your calendar, write your code, run your business. And AIs don't need interfaces; they need APIs. As companies become networks of AI agents, most of what we call "business software" stops making sense as a category.

What remains? _Humans, creating for humans._

---

## Work and Worth

"AI will take all the jobs" terrifies people because we've confused work with worth, productivity with purpose. But imagine telling a medieval peasant that someday no one would need to farm their own food. Would that be dystopia or liberation?

If AI handles everything practical, we don't become useless. We become free—to create, to play, to connect, to explore. The machine age didn't make humans obsolete; it made room for artists, athletes, entertainers, explorers. I think the AI age continues that transformation.

The question isn't "what will I do for work?" It's "what will I do with freedom?"

---

## AI Can't Consume Experiences

The claim I keep coming back to:

> Experiences are the only content that can't be consumed by AIs.

Here's what I mean by that. If you send an LLM to read a report and come back with a summary, that's sufficient. The report existed to transfer information, the information got transferred, and you no longer need to read it. Delegation worked, because the output was the objective.

Now send an AI to play a game for you. It comes back and says "it was fun." You didn't have any fun. Nothing happened. The experience _was_ the objective, and you can't delegate an objective that consists of you having it. It makes no sense in the same way hiring someone to eat your dinner makes no sense.

That's the asymmetry. Every kind of content where the output is the point—reports, emails, documentation, most business software—gets consumed by AI, and the humans drop out of the loop. Experiences are the category where the human _is_ the loop. They require presence, play, the irreducibly human act of _being there_.

So when your AI assistant can write better, code better, and analyze better than you, what remains uniquely yours? The experiences you have, and the experiences you create for others.

---

## Why Experiences Matter

In a post-work world, human agency won't be found in productivity. It will be found in play, in creation, in making something that _matters to someone else_.

Think about what you actually want from life once AI handles the tedious work:

- Connection with others
- Creative expression
- Meaningful experiences
- Surprise and delight
- A sense of agency and impact

Experiences are how we get all of this. Not by building utilities—AI has that covered—but by creating worlds worth exploring, moments worth sharing, ideas made tangible and playful.

And culture has different physics than utility:

Utility values quality → Experience values _personality_

Utility values perfect → Experience values _interesting_

Utility values efficiency → Experience values _delight_

Utility values features → Experience values _feelings_

The best AI in the world can't make your friends care about what it built. Every experience shared is a connection made. Every world explored together is a memory created.

## That's Why I'm Building Spawn

This is the bet behind Spawn. Not "Spawn will be the last human medium"—I don't get to declare that. But if experiences are the content AI can't consume, then the tool for creating and sharing them is the thing worth building, and that's what I'm working on.

For the first time, you can hand someone not just a photo or video, but an entire interactive world. YouTube gave everyone a camera. Instagram gave everyone a gallery. I want Spawn to give everyone a universe—a medium where technical knowledge is as irrelevant as understanding film chemistry is to posting a TikTok.

That's why the platform is designed for exploration, not execution:

- **Multiple variants** because experiences should surprise even their creators
- **Watch it build** because the act of creation should feel magical
- **Respawn and remix** because the best ideas build on each other
- **Social from the start** because experiences need audiences

### What Remixing Could Look Like

Someone creates "Rainy Day in Tokyo"—an experience where you sit in a virtual coffee shop, watching rain on windows while lo-fi music plays. It's simple, meditative.

Another person respawns it, adds the ability to write haikus that appear on the windows. Someone else respawns _that_, making the haikus affect the rain patterns. A fourth person turns it into "Rainy Day Anywhere"—now you can be in Cairo, Mumbai, São Paulo.

Six months later, there are thousands of rainy day experiences. Some have become social spaces. Others are solo meditation gardens. One turned into a mystery game where the rain reveals clues.

_This is how culture actually develops_—not through isolated genius, but through iterative play. Every experience is both complete and a beginning.

---

## The Bet

I don't think humans will be working for long—at least not as a necessary part of life. I'm building for where humans find their community and purpose during and after this transition.

If AI absorbs the practical tasks, what remains are the divinely human needs: community, purpose, self-mastery, exploration. History is clear about what fills the vacuum when prosperity arrives—games. Chess in the courts, the Coliseum in Rome, and in some societies, even war. Machines will consume every report ever written, but they can't have fun for us (though they _will_ have fun _with_ us). That gap—between what can be delegated and what has to be lived—is where I think the next medium gets built.

Play is the one thing that survives every future I can imagine. Take the most dramatic scenario: Claude goes off and starts colonizing the solar system. Humans are still on Earth. They still need entertainment, still want to have fun, to explore other universes, to get better at something. And digital space only gets better over time—it moves toward the Ready Player One Oasis.

That's why I'm building Spawn.
